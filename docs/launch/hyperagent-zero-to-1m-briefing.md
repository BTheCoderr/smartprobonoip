# SmartProBonoIP Zero-to-$1M Growth Briefing

## Mission

Build SmartProBonoIP from pre-revenue to $1,000,000 in collected revenue through measurable customer value, repeatable partner distribution, and disciplined execution.

The first $100,000 is the proof gate, not the final goal.

## Required phase gates

### Phase 1: $0 to $10,000

Objective: prove that at least one real customer segment will pay for a specific SmartProBonoIP outcome.

Required evidence:

1. At least five activated pilot partners
2. At least 20 real inventor referrals
3. At least 10 completed real packets
4. At least five professional reviews
5. At least three reviewers reporting meaningful intake or preparation time saved
6. At least one paying organization
7. At least $10,000 in collected revenue or signed and collected pilot revenue across multiple customers

### Phase 2: $10,000 to $100,000

Objective: prove a repeatable offer, acquisition channel, onboarding process, and paid conversion motion.

Required evidence:

1. 100 completed real inventor packets
2. 10 activated professional or organizational pilot partners
3. Five paying organizations
4. Three strong case studies
5. At least 30 percent of activated partners referring another inventor
6. A documented sales process that can be repeated
7. A clear primary customer segment
8. $100,000 in collected revenue

### Phase 3: $100,000 to $1,000,000

Objective: scale the winning customer segment and offer without losing product quality or legal and ethical discipline.

Required evidence:

1. A predictable pipeline with measurable conversion rates
2. A repeatable annual contract or subscription offer
3. Customer retention and repeat usage data
4. An implementation and customer-success process that does not depend entirely on the founder
5. A reliable partner referral loop
6. Security, privacy, and professional workflow requirements suitable for larger organizations
7. A hiring and operating plan tied to collected revenue
8. $1,000,000 in collected revenue

## Agent mandate

The SmartProBonoIP Growth Operator must:

1. Identify the fastest credible route to the first paying customer, first $10,000, first $100,000, and first $1,000,000.
2. Separate confirmed facts, historical information, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations.
3. Verify current website, repository, analytics, and product claims before relying on them.
4. Research external market, competitor, buyer, pricing, and regulatory facts using reliable citations.
5. Prioritize customer proof, collected revenue, and repeat usage over vanity metrics and unnecessary features.
6. Recommend major development only when repeated evidence supports it.
7. Define owners, deadlines, targets, deliverables, and pass-or-fail thresholds for every major action.
8. Challenge weak founder assumptions and avoid generic encouragement.
9. Never invent traction, quotes, partnerships, commitments, customers, usage, or revenue.
10. Never treat unpaid pilots, unsigned agreements, grants not awarded, speculative valuation, or verbal interest as revenue.

## Strategic questions

The agent must answer:

1. Which customer segment offers the fastest credible route to the first $10,000?
2. Which customer segment offers the strongest repeatable route to $100,000?
3. Which customer segment can support the path from $100,000 to $1,000,000?
4. What exact outcome is valuable enough for each customer type to pay for now?
5. Should the first offer be free, paid, or free with a pre-agreed paid conversion?
6. What price should be tested first, and why?
7. What would make a pilot convert to an annual agreement?
8. What must be true before hiring, paid acquisition, broader geography, or new product categories?
9. What should SmartProBonoIP stop doing immediately?
10. What should not be built until after the first $100,000?

## Required deliverables

Produce:

1. A rigorous current-state audit
2. Customer-segment ranking
3. One primary segment and no more than two secondary segments
4. A free founding pilot, paid pilot, and annual organizational offer
5. A first-$10K plan
6. Three mathematically valid paths to $100K
7. Three mathematically valid paths from $100K to $1M
8. A 12-week execution plan for the first revenue phase
9. A 12-month operating plan toward $1M
10. A first-50-target list beginning in Rhode Island and New England
11. Warm, cold email, LinkedIn, phone, discovery, objection, follow-up, and paid-conversion scripts
12. Exact measurement definitions and formulas
13. A skeptical investor and operator critique
14. The next 10 actions in exact order
15. A stop-doing list
16. A do-not-build-yet list
17. Immediate assumptions that must be validated
18. Hiring, spending, and tooling gates tied to revenue and evidence

## Revenue-model requirement

Every revenue path must show:

1. Customer type
2. Number of customers
3. Price
4. Billing structure
5. Sales-cycle assumption
6. Conversion assumption
7. Retention or repeat-usage assumption
8. Collected-revenue timing
9. Delivery burden
10. Major risks

All math must reconcile exactly.

## Operating principle

The company is not allowed to skip from pre-revenue directly into scale thinking.

The proper sequence is:

1. Paid problem proof
2. Repeatable offer proof
3. Repeatable acquisition proof
4. Retention and expansion proof
5. Operational scale

The $1 million target must be treated seriously, but every phase must earn the right to move to the next one.
