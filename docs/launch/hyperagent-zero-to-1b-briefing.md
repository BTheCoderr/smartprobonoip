# SmartProBonoIP Zero-to-$1B Master Briefing

## North star

Build SmartProBonoIP into a category-defining company worth at least $1 billion.

The billion-dollar goal is a long-term company-value target, not a claim that $1 billion in revenue is immediately achievable or guaranteed.

The company must earn each phase through collected revenue, repeat usage, customer retention, measurable professional value, and defensible distribution.

## Phase gates

### Gate 1: $0 to $10,000 collected revenue

Prove that at least one real customer segment will pay for a specific SmartProBonoIP outcome.

Required evidence:

1. Five activated pilot partners
2. Twenty real inventor referrals
3. Ten completed real packets
4. Five professional reviews
5. Three reviewers reporting meaningful intake or preparation time saved
6. At least one paying organization
7. $10,000 in collected revenue

### Gate 2: $10,000 to $100,000 collected revenue

Prove a repeatable offer, sales motion, onboarding process, and paid conversion path.

Required evidence:

1. One hundred completed real inventor packets
2. Ten activated professional or organizational partners
3. Five paying organizations
4. Three strong case studies
5. At least thirty percent of activated partners referring another inventor
6. A documented sales process
7. A clearly selected primary customer segment
8. $100,000 in collected revenue

### Gate 3: $100,000 to $1 million collected revenue

Scale the winning segment and offer without losing quality, safety, or customer value.

Required evidence:

1. Predictable pipeline conversion rates
2. Repeatable paid pilot and annual contract structures
3. Customer retention and repeat-usage data
4. A customer-success process that is not entirely founder-dependent
5. A reliable referral or channel loop
6. Security and privacy controls suitable for organizations
7. A hiring plan tied to collected revenue
8. $1 million in collected revenue

### Gate 4: $1 million to $10 million annual recurring revenue

Prove category leadership in early-stage intellectual property intake and preparation.

Required evidence:

1. Strong retention and expansion revenue
2. Multiple repeatable acquisition channels
3. Professional workflow adoption beyond individual inventors
4. Organization-wide contracts
5. Institutional integrations
6. Reliable implementation and support operations
7. Clear unit economics
8. A defensible data and workflow advantage

### Gate 5: $10 million to $100 million annual recurring revenue

Expand from a strong entry wedge into the broader intellectual-property preparation and workflow infrastructure market.

Potential expansion areas must be validated before investment and may include:

1. Invention disclosure management
2. University and incubator IP intake
3. Professional intake workflow software
4. Trademark and design preparation
5. Licensing and commercialization preparation
6. Portfolio and deadline coordination
7. Referral and professional marketplace infrastructure
8. International preparation workflows

No expansion area is approved merely because it appears on this list.

### Gate 6: Billion-dollar company value

The billion-dollar outcome should be pursued through category leadership, durable revenue growth, strong retention, defensible workflow and data assets, institutional distribution, and trusted professional adoption.

The agent must never assume a valuation multiple. It must model multiple valuation scenarios and identify the revenue, growth, retention, market position, and capital requirements that would make each scenario credible.

## Agent mandate

The SmartProBonoIP Growth Operator must:

1. Work backward from the billion-dollar company goal without skipping proof stages.
2. Prioritize the next revenue gate rather than distant speculation.
3. Identify the fastest credible path to the first customer, first $10,000, first $100,000, first $1 million, and later scale milestones.
4. Separate confirmed facts, historical information, assumptions, hypotheses, and recommendations.
5. Verify the current live website, repository, product capabilities, analytics, and customer evidence.
6. Research market, competitors, buyers, pricing, legal constraints, and comparable businesses using reliable citations.
7. Recommend major development only when repeated customer evidence or measurable bottlenecks justify it.
8. Define owners, deadlines, numerical targets, deliverables, and pass-or-fail thresholds.
9. Challenge weak founder assumptions and avoid generic encouragement.
10. Never invent traction, partnerships, quotes, commitments, customers, usage, or revenue.
11. Never count unpaid pilots, unsigned agreements, unawarded grants, speculative valuation, or verbal interest as revenue.
12. Protect confidentiality and avoid requesting sensitive invention details when sanitized product evidence is sufficient.

## Immediate planning horizon

The first operating plan must focus on the next twelve weeks and the first $100,000.

The agent must also show how success at $100,000 could logically create a path to:

1. $1 million in collected revenue
2. $10 million in annual recurring revenue
3. $100 million in annual recurring revenue
4. A credible billion-dollar company-value scenario

Long-range projections must be clearly labeled as scenarios rather than forecasts.

## Revenue-model standard

Every revenue path must show:

1. Customer type
2. Number of customers
3. Price
4. Billing structure
5. Sales-cycle assumption
6. Conversion assumption
7. Retention or repeat-usage assumption
8. Collected-revenue timing
9. Delivery burden
10. Major risks

All math must reconcile exactly.

## Billion-dollar scenario standard

Every billion-dollar scenario must show:

1. Revenue level
2. Growth rate
3. Gross margin assumption
4. Retention assumption
5. Customer concentration risk
6. Market-size assumption
7. Defensibility
8. Required team and capital
9. Valuation multiple assumption
10. Why that multiple could or could not be justified

## Operating sequence

The company is not allowed to skip stages.

The required sequence is:

1. Paid problem proof
2. Repeatable offer proof
3. Repeatable acquisition proof
4. Retention and expansion proof
5. Operational scale
6. Category leadership
7. Billion-dollar company-value credibility

The long-term goal is one billion dollars in company value. The immediate job is earning the first paying customers and the first $100,000 through real proof.
